# Four missed edges force a spanning subgraph of stars

Take a minimum normalized monotone circuit with at most 58 gates for the deleted projective plane in [L-meta-9737](https://pequalnp.com/desk/row?gap=L-meta-9737). Let $H$ be the union of its [admissible raw AND stars](first-star-cover.md). If $H$ misses exactly four edges, every point and every line has a neighbor in $H$. Thus the earlier restrictions for the [four-edge case with no isolated line](first-star-thirteen.md) hold throughout the four-edge case. In particular, there are exactly twelve centers, at most one of which is a point.

## Support and comparison bounds at an isolated vertex

Suppose $H$ isolates $v$ and put $X=N_G(v)$. There is only one isolated vertex, since isolating two vertices of original degree at least three takes at least five deletions. All $x\in X$ have nonempty projected raw supports, and these supports are pairwise disjoint. A center label can occur at only one raw vertex. A common leaf label for $x,x'\in X$ would give them a second common neighbor besides $v$.

Use the transposed union network and the notation of the [projection count](first-star-cover.md#projection-to-the-star-incidences). Write $\sigma,c,w$ for the number of oriented incidences, distinct star centers, and raw-reachable operand positions of nonadmissible gates. There are at least $\sigma+c-24$ retained unions required for the 24 nonempty projected raw supports. There are at most 34 unions in total. A union with both operands erased is called pure erased. A union with exactly one erased operand is a mixed attachment. Both kinds are disjoint from the retained count. A path carrying an erased position to a nonempty projected raw support has a first mixed attachment. For the vertices of $X$, any such attachment can feed at most one of them, since its retained operand would otherwise yield a common projected label.

When $w\le3$ there is a unique nonadmissible gate $g$ with two raw-reachable operands $a,b$. It must fire first on all the edges from $v$ to $X$. If one of $a,b$ is missing from $D(v)$, it must reach all of $X$, costing at least $|X|$ mixed attachments. In each application below this exceeds the available erased unions. Thus $a,b\in D(v)$. The support $D(v)$ itself consists entirely of erased positions. If $D(v)=\{a,b\}$, there must be at least two pure erased unions used to compute it in a minimum circuit. With just one, the forward OR-only paths from $v$ to the two operands of $g$ have only one fork and no other destinations. The [absorption or factoring reduction](first-star-cover.md#spanning-in-the-three-edge-case) saves a gate. If $D(v)$ contains a third position, it also requires two pure erased unions to connect its three distinct sources.

We will compare the rejecting singleton run at $v$ with the positive tests at $(v,x)$. Use consuming evaluation, and write $F$ for the set of gates firing on the singleton. On every positive test there must be a first additional gate, since no raw support supplies the output. Its operands are in the singleton closure or the added raw support $D(x)$. At least one is supplied by $D(x)$, and neither operand was consumed by $F$, since each operand position is read by exactly one gate. In particular, if the only available additions outside the already fired gates are good leaf positions, the additional gate needs a center left in the singleton's terminal support. Different $x\in X$ cannot use the same center for this first difference, because their projected leaf sets are disjoint.

For the singleton run, let $K$ count the good center positions and the raw-unreachable operand positions currently present. It starts at zero. Every fired gate except a nonadmissible gate with both operands raw-reachable consumes at least one counted position. In the cases where every union is used for a raw support and no raw support repeats a center label, a destination adds at most one counted position. Indeed a nonsingleton destination is contained in a raw support. In that situation $K$ is bounded by the number of nonadmissible gates with two raw-reachable operands that fire on the singleton.

## An isolated point

If $v$ is a point, the [four-edge restrictions](first-star-thirteen.md) give $c=12$, $w=2$, and $\sigma=44$, since no line is isolated. The 32 retained unions leave at most two erased unions, fewer than the three or four neighbors of $v$. Thus $D(v)=\{a,b\}$, using two pure erased unions. The count exhausts the 34 unions. There are no mixed attachments and $a,b$ do not appear at other raw vertices. This puts all four missed edges at $v$. The retained count is tight and every union contributes to a raw support. The [equality argument](first-star-three.md#consequences-of-equality) gives one gate per center and at most one center per destination.

The [twelve-center cover count](first-star-four.md#cover-choices-with-12-centers) leaves all line centers or all point centers when a point is isolated. In the all-point case, every $x\in X$ contributes only good leaf positions beyond the singleton. Its first additional gate needs a terminal center, but $K\le1$ because only $g$ has two raw-reachable erased operands. In the all-line case, $x\in X$ contributes exactly its center $C_x$. Following $g$ on the singleton, a center-bearing destination is a singleton, a nonraw position is a singleton, and the output cannot appear. So the run on $(v,x)$ can continue only if $g$ supplies the target leaf $L_x$. All supplied leaves lie in one raw point support. Since $v$ is isolated in $H$, any such support contains at most one of the four required leaves by the common-neighbor bound. Both cases are impossible.

## An isolated line

If $v$ is a line, all four missed edges are at $v$, and all eleven other lines keep their four edges. A matching covering these eleven lines is obtained by restricting one of the full line matchings to them, so $c\ge11$. The bound $s\ge \sigma+c-1+w$ gives $c\le13$ and $w\le4$.

If $c=11$, all eleven active lines are centers. In fact, omitting $k>0$ active lines would leave $k$ point centers to cover $4k$ incidences. Each such point would have degree four into the omitted lines, requiring $6k\le\binom{k}{2}$ by the common-neighbor bound, which is impossible for $k\le11$. If $c=12$, at most one active line could be omitted. To see this, $k$ omitted lines would use $k+1$ point centers and $4k$ incidences. At degrees $d_t\le4$, the inequality $\binom{d_t}{2}\ge 6-3(4-d_t)$ gives $\binom{k}{2}\ge6(k-1)$, excluding $2\le k\le11$. One omitted line cannot retain all four neighbors with only two point centers. Thus $c=12$ consists of all active lines and one point.

### At least twelve centers

Here $w\le3$ and the retained count leaves at most two erased unions. As above $D(v)$ contains $a,b$ and uses at least two pure erased unions. This already exceeds the allowance when $c=13$. When $c=12$ the two pure erased unions and the 32 retained unions exhaust the count. Every erased position is in $D(v)$ since none can enter a live raw support without a mixed attachment. There are no repeated center positions and every destination adds at most one position counted by $K$. Only $g$ has two raw-reachable erased operands, so $K\le1$ on the singleton. At least three of the four $x\in X$ are noncenters and each adds only good leaves beyond the singleton. These would need at least three terminal centers, a contradiction.

### Eleven centers and four raw-reachable erased positions

Here the projection-and-erasure count is tight. All unions contribute to raw supports, each center has one gate, and the [tight attachment count](first-star-four.md#budget-and-saturation) applies. In particular, each erased position is private to the isolated raw vertex or enters the live raw supports through a single mixed attachment. Hence each erased position is present in the raw support of at most one of the four neighbors.

Let $J$ count the nonadmissible gates with two raw-reachable operands that fire on the singleton $v$. We have $J\ge1$. Otherwise each such gate would require at least one erased position from $x$ to fire initially on $(v,x)$, and could do so for at most one neighbor. There are at most two such gates and all four tests need a first gate. The bound on counted positions now gives at most $J$ centers in the terminal singleton support. Each neighbor adds only good leaves and possibly erased positions. At most $J$ neighbors can have a good gate as a first difference. Among the erased positions, the $2J$ operands of the fired nonadmissible gates cannot cause a first difference. Only $4-2J$ other erased positions may contribute, each to at most one neighbor. This covers at most $4-J<4$ neighbors.

### Eleven centers and at most three raw-reachable erased positions

The retained count leaves at most three erased unions, fewer than the four neighbors, so $D(v)$ contains $a,b$. At least two pure erased unions feed $D(v)$. When $w=3$, let $d$ be the third erased position. If $d$ is absent from $D(v)$, it must use a mixed attachment elsewhere. This exhausts the union count and allows $d$ to reach at most one neighbor. Every destination then increases $K$ by at most one before consumption, and $g$ is the sole gate that can increase $K$ overall. In the terminal singleton support $K\le1$. For the at least three neighbors who cannot supply $d$, the first difference needs a good center, which cannot serve even two of them.

It remains that all erased positions lie in $D(v)$. All four neighbors then add only good leaves beyond the singleton. We bound $K$ by two using the one union still available after paying for 31 retained unions and the two pure erased unions. The 31 retained unions counted here have projected supports containing at least two leaf labels at live raw points. If an active line $\ell$ instead reaches $q_\ell$ actual center slots, this requires $q_\ell-1$ additional unions to connect the distinct slots into its raw support, after ignoring the erased positions. These unions have only the center label of $\ell$ in their projection. They cannot be shared between raw lines or included in the 31 retained unions counted for the points.

Consequently the additional unions for repeated center slots and the unions unused by any raw support total at most one. If a line has two actual center slots, there is no union outside the raw supports, and any destination that adds two counted positions adds those same two center slots. If every line has one slot, there is at most one union outside the raw supports. It can unite two raw-ancestral supports or single positions, each with at most one counted position. Again any destination that adds two counted positions uses the same pair. All other destinations add at most one. The first use of the distinguished pair costs at most one increase above the bound $K\le1$ from $g$. If the pair is first emitted by $g$, it simply gives $K\le2$ at that step. Any repeat use adds no new counted position, because a previously emitted position remains present unless its gate fired, and later gates cannot emit consumed positions. Thus $K\le2$ in the terminal singleton support. This cannot give a first difference for all four neighbors.

This rules out an isolated line as well. A [follow-up](first-star-four-exclusion.md) excludes the remaining spanning cases with four missed edges. The question of existence at size 58 is still open.
