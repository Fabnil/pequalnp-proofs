# Where the spare union can occur with five uncovered edges

Suppose a minimum normalized circuit of size at most 58 for [L-meta-9737](https://pequalnp.com/desk/row?gap=L-meta-9737) has exactly five edges outside its admissible raw stars. The [preceding bounds](first-star-five-fourteen.md) give size 58, thirteen distinct centers, singly oriented retained edges, and two nonadmissible operand positions $a,b$ that are raw-reachable. They belong to one AND $g$ that fires first on every missing edge. We show that the centers must be all twelve lines and one point. Both erased positions have one attachment each, and $g$ fires on a singleton. If singleton triggers are lines, the spare union must be outside the raw ancestries.

Let $A$ count the unions with an erased operand in raw ancestries, $R$ repeated actual center slots, and $E$ unions outside raw ancestries. The [count with individual center slots](first-star-five-cover.md#tracking-additional-positions) gives $2\le A$ and $A+R+E\le3$. After $g$, neither $a$ nor $b$ can be emitted again. The tracked positions are good centers and raw-unreachable operands. A singleton noncenter can finish with at most $1+R+E$ tracks after firing $g$. As long as one track is available, the next gate is fixed or the run stops. A raw target center supplies no choice until a destination emits its missing leaf.

## Three unions with erased operands

Here $R=E=0$. If a mixed attachment carries both $a,b$, the [one-fork reduction](first-star-five-cover.md#singleton-triggers-with-all-line-centers) requires two pure unions, leaving just one mixed attachment. Otherwise the attachments for the two slots are separate, and one slot, say $b$, has just one attachment. Write $V$ for the raw set of $b$ and $U$ for that of $a$. The set $V$ is in the assigned leaves of a center $c$ on the opposite side. A single raw vertex could not account for five misses. Every missing edge meets $V$, and the opposite endpoints are distinct. The singleton triggers form $S=U\cap V$.

The same noncenter condition used in the [saturated geometry check](first-star-five-thirteen.md#equality-and-the-possible-placements-of-centers) holds even when a missing test has no singleton trigger. Suppose $v\in V$ is a noncenter with two missing noncenter neighbors. After $g$, the tests share its destination and the good leaves $B_v$ of raw $v$. The added leaf sets are disjoint since the neighbors already share $v$. The common baseline has at most one track, so it must accept. If $v\in S$, this accepts its singleton. Otherwise every opposite vertex supplying $a$ must belong to $N_G(v)$, since pairing it with $v$ starts $g$ and contains the accepting baseline. Each group supplying opposite-side $a$ meets $N_G(v)$ at most once. It is a singleton or in one star's leaf set, whose center is different from the noncenter $v$. Thus the two missed neighbors would use both attachments for $a$, each a singleton group on the opposite side. Those two opposite vertices account for at most two misses in all. This contradicts five.

The geometry check therefore still leaves all points as centers or all lines and one point. It needs only that the five deletions are at leaves of $c$, that $H$ spans, and that a noncenter cannot have two missing noncenter neighbors. It does not need the points or lines in $V$ actually to trigger $g$ on singletons.

### All points are centers

The side of $V$ is lines, since each point group is a singleton. Any point-side attachment for $a$ can account for at most one missed edge, so at least four misses use singleton triggers. Adding the point to one of those triggering lines adds only its center beyond the consumed $a,b$. Thus each target leaf must be reached on its line singleton. There is one control sequence to a common leaf-only destination, because the raw point supports contain only centers and possible erased marks, and the raw lines contain no centers. The destination can supply at most one missing point per trigger line by the common-neighbor bound for lines. Hence four misses need four distinct singleton triggers. But $V$ lies in one point's at most four assigned leaves. It follows that all of $V$ belongs to $S$, making all five tests ordinary singleton-trigger tests, which is impossible.

### All lines and one point are centers

Write $z$ for the point center and $J$ for the lines assigned by $z$, hence carrying $L_z$. Raw line centers can carry $L_z$ but no line leaves. The point center can carry the line leaves $B_z$, none of which is a leaf of a line in $J$.

If $V$ consists of lines, it lies in $J$. Each such line retains its edge from the $z$ star and a different edge of its own star, so at least three lines have missing edges. All their missing points are noncenters. Substituting any other line in $V$ for the target line gives a nonedge and preserves the extra $L_z$ and raw $b$. This can lose the necessary raw $a$ only when substituting for a line in $S$. If $S$ has at least two lines, substitution within $S$ works there too. Hence all but at most one of these target lines must have their center used. The target leaves cannot be emitted with a center, and the common leaf-only destination can give at most one leaf of a line through $z$. This cannot cover the at least two required targets.

Suppose instead that $V$ consists of points. If $U$ is entirely on the point side, all misses are singleton-trigger tests. The [one-point-center argument at equality](first-star-five-thirteen.md#point-triggers) uses only the single star group, one orientation per edge, $w=2$, and destinations contained in raw supports, and excludes this situation too.

If $a$ has no point-side attachment, its two line groups must cover five distinct missing lines. One group must contain all four lines of $J$ and the other a line outside $J$, since each group is a singleton or in $J$. The four $J$ tests are at noncenter points. Each requires its target by substituting a different $J$ line with the same extra positions. No center-bearing destination can offer these target leaves, and the common leaf-only destination supplies at most one of them.

There remains one attachment for $a$ on each side. Consider missing lines in $J$. For two such tests with the same raw $a$ status at the line, substitution would force both to use their own target center. They could only obtain their missing leaves from the same leaf-only destination, which supplies at most one. There are thus at most two missing lines in $J$. If the line attachment is outside $J$, it is a singleton, so there is only one raw $a$ status in $J$ and at most one other miss on the singleton line carrying $a$. Thus at least three missing lines have just their center as raw support and have points in $S$.

If $z$ is not in $S$, those three tests have noncenter points. Each of their good gates is later than $g$ and accepts from its destination alone. In fact it has at least two retained noncenter points outside $V$, whose tests start at that gate and have disjoint extra leaves, which cannot both distinguish a rejecting destination with one waiting center. A point singleton in $S$ can emit line leaves only with one offer of $C_z$ or with its final leaf-only destination. Each such destination can contribute to at most one of these targets, since it would give a false positive at a different point of $S$ and it cannot share two missing lines with a fixed point. If $z\in S$, there are no misses in $J$. The point $z$ keeps both its edge assigned from $c$ and an edge of its own star, so at least three misses are at noncenters. At most one of those can use the singleton line carrying $a$ outside $J$. Thus at least two have raw lines with just their center and accepting good-gate destinations. Their lines do not contain $z$, so their leaves must both come from the one final leaf-only destination. This is impossible by the same comparison.

## Two unions with erased operands

The erasure count is tight. Each position has one attachment and the raw sets must intersect, since disjoint sets cover at most a matching of size four. The singleton triggers $S$ belong to one star's assigned leaves. A noncenter trigger can have at most two missing noncenter neighbors, by the waiting-center bound. It cannot retain just one edge either. Its only actual good leaf is then that of the one retained edge, which belongs to one good gate by unique orientation. That leaf is shared through the mixed attachment with the other members of $S$. Thus its whole raw support is contained in that of any other trigger, violating [raw-support dominance](first-star-five-saturation.md#setup-and-raw-support-dominance). The [geometry checker](first_star_five_geometry.py) with the extra-union option gives zero omitted lines for point triggers, and zero, one, two or twelve for line triggers.

### One or two omitted lines

With one omitted line $u$, it retains exactly two chosen points and misses two noncenter points. Its singleton needs two waiting line centers. Each waiting line meets $u$ at one of the missing points, so cannot contain either chosen point. Its raw support consists only of its center slots and possibly $a,b$, already present in singleton $u$. Thus both waiting line centers would have to be split to avoid raw-support dominance, although $R\le1$.

With two omitted lines $u,v$, there are at most four incidences to the three chosen points. Each omitted line is in $S$ and retains at least two, so both retain two and together they retain all four incidences. They each miss two noncenter points, and both singleton runs must end with exactly two tracked positions, their two waiting line centers.

Compare their runs after $g$ in gate order. A first differing firing would have to be at a point center, since only the raw good point leaves differ. That center position reached both runs from a common emission, so it remains unconsumed in the run that does not fire the gate. This conflicts with its two final tracks being line centers. Thus the runs have the same waiting centers. Each such line supports a missing point on both $u$ and $v$ and cannot contain any chosen point, since each chosen point is on at least one of $u,v$. Once again both waiting centers would need to be split.

### All points are centers

The trigger side is lines and each opposite target point supplies only its center positions. Keep the unsplit point targets, at least four of the five. Their trigger singletons must reach their missing leaves. Each kept target has two retained noncenter neighbors outside $S$ with disjoint extra leaf sets after its good gate.

If $E=0$, the baseline from the good gate's destination either accepts or stops at its initial support. A raw point support has only its own group of center slots, without leaves, and a raw line support has leaves without centers. A raw-unreachable singleton cannot continue. One group of centers cannot distinguish the two noncenter neighbors, so the destination accepts. On a line singleton no point's whole raw support can be reached. Since at most one point splits, at most one actual center slot can be reached. The single control sequence thus gives one common leaf-only destination. It supplies at most one missing point per trigger line, so at least two trigger lines reach it and cross a kept target to a nonedge.

If $E=1$ there is no split. A rejecting good-gate baseline at any target needs two waiting centers to distinguish its retained neighbors. The extra union must supply a pair of good centers. A raw-unreachable pair would require consuming two tracks and reduce the bound of two, and a pending unreachable position would leave fewer than two center tracks. The extra union cannot then be used on any line singleton, since an unsplit point center is the whole raw support of that point. The singletons could only obtain one ordinary leaf destination of size at most four. If all targets instead have accepting destinations, the line singletons use no good centers and get the same new leaves from $g$ and possible continuations on raw-unreachable pairs. Crossing with another trigger line again accepts a nonedge.

### The remaining placement

All lines and one point $z$ are centers. We must have $R+E=1$. With no repeated center or external union, the [tight-case argument](first-star-five-thirteen.md) applies to the same raw groups and destinations.

We can also exclude line triggers with $E=0,R=1$. All lines in $S$ share one actual leaf slot of $z$, because the common attachment uses one retained label and each line obtains just one leaf slot from the uniquely oriented edge at $z$. Each line keeps an edge assigned from $z$ and one of its own, so at least three trigger lines have misses. Their missing points are noncenters. Every such test must use a good gate of its own line. Otherwise substituting another line in $S$ changes only unread center positions and accepts a nonedge. No center-bearing destination can emit a target leaf, since the lines in $S$ receive their edge from $z$ and $B_z$ has none of those line leaves.

Before a target is enabled there is just one control sequence until either a leaf-only destination or the one split-pair offer that can introduce two tracked positions. That pair can add new controls only on its first emission. From it each control path can end in a leaf-only destination or in one raw-unreachable position. If two paths end in mates they merge to give at most one later path. The raw target centers remain disabled until a leaf-only emission supplies their leaf, so they add no control before then. At most two fixed leaf destinations can first enable the required targets, each containing at most one target line in $S$ by the common-neighbor bound with $z$. There are at least three different target lines.

The remaining five-edge branch has all lines and one point as centers, two erased unions, and either an external union or a split star with point triggers. The existence of a circuit of size 58 remains open.
