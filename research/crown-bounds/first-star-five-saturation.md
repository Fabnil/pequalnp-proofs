# Excluding saturation with twelve centers and five uncovered edges

For the deleted plane of [L-meta-9737](https://pequalnp.com/desk/row?gap=L-meta-9737), let $H$ be the union of the admissible raw AND stars of a minimum normalized circuit of size at most 58. Suppose $H$ misses exactly five edges and every line has a neighbor in $H$. Equality in the projection-and-erasure bound with twelve distinct centers is impossible. In particular, at most three operand positions outside the stars can be raw-reachable, and a single nonadmissible AND must fire first on all five missing edges.

## Setup and raw-support dominance

By the [five-edge saturation analysis](first-star-five.md#twelve-centers), the case to exclude has all twelve lines as centers, $H$ spanning, and two nonadmissible gates $g_1,g_2$ with both operands raw-reachable. These account for the four erased raw-reachable positions. The remaining nonadmissible gates have two raw-unreachable operands. Raw line $\ell$ has good position $C_\ell$ and possibly erased positions. Raw point $x$ has good leaf positions $B_x$ and possibly erased positions. Write $h_\ell$ for the good AND with operands $C_\ell,L_\ell$.

At equality every nonsingleton destination is contained in one raw support. A destination adds at most one tracked position, where the tracked positions are the good centers and all raw-unreachable operands. The two gates $g_1,g_2$ can each increase the number of tracked positions by at most one. Each other gate that fires consumes at least one tracked position.

Write $D(v)$ for the raw support and use ordinary closure when discussing which positions are reached. If the closure of singleton $u$ contains $D(v)$ for $v\ne u$, then $N_G(v)\subseteq N_G(u)$. Indeed adding any raw neighbor of $v$ to the singleton at $u$ would reproduce a positive test from $v$. This is impossible because the neighborhoods on either side have size at least three and pairwise intersection at most one, and the neighborhoods on opposite sides lie on different sides. In particular a singleton point cannot reach the center of a line whose entire raw support is its center position. Call such lines clean.

At saturation, the raw set for each erased position is either one line or a subset of the assigned point leaves of one line. Thus each of $g_1,g_2$ has one of three types according to the sides of its two raw sets. With two point-side sets, its initial point-line firings require the point to belong to their intersection $S_i$, itself a subset of one line's assigned leaves. Among missing edges, at most one at any given line can fire it initially. With a set on each side, the gate can fire initially on at most one missing edge. Its one raw line either is the center of the point set, so none of those edges are missing, or meets that set in at most one point. With two line-side sets, its initial point-line firings require both sets to be the same singleton line.

## Both gates have line-side sets

In fact, if neither gate has two point-side sets, both must have equal singleton line sets for their two operands. A line accounts for at most three missing edges since $H$ spans, and one gate with sets on different sides could add at most one. Thus the five missing edges are on two distinct lines $u,v$, each a singleton trigger for its own gate. One of these lines, say $u$, has three missing edges.

The three missing points add pairwise disjoint good leaf sets to the singleton at $u$. No erased position comes from a point, and the points cannot share a leaf of another line because they already share $u$. The [first additional gate](first-star-spanning.md#support-and-comparison-bounds-at-an-isolated-vertex) needs a waiting center for each of them. The singleton starts with one tracked position $C_u$ and must end with at least three centers. This forces both nonadmissible gates to fire on that singleton, and every firing to emit a tracked position. Otherwise the final count is at most two.

The two erased operands of the gate at $v$ are absent from the raw support at $u$. Each must therefore be emitted with a tracked position on the singleton at $u$. Such a destination must be a subset of $D(v)$, since the erased operand is raw-reachable only at $v$. Its tracked position is necessarily $C_v$. The singleton at $u$ then reaches all of $D(v)$, contrary to raw-support dominance.

## One gate has two point-side sets and the other has a line-side set

Write $g$ for the first type and $f$ for the other. The singleton triggers of $g$ form a set $S$ of assigned point leaves of a line $c$. At least two missing edges are at clean lines. If both erased operands of $f$ belong to the same line, at most three missing edges are there. If only one operand belongs to a line, at most two missing edges are there, one from each gate's possible initial firings. If the two operands belong to distinct lines, all missing edges trigger $g$ and each of the two unclean lines accounts for at most one. In both of these last cases at least three missing edges are at clean lines.

Each clean missing line has exactly one missing edge, at a point $x\in S$. The other three neighbors lie outside $S$. Adding that clean line to the singleton $x$ adds just its center $C_\ell$, so the accepting test must first differ at $h_\ell$. The singleton at $x$ reaches $L_\ell$, and $h_\ell$ is later than $g$.

On such point singletons, $g$ is the only initial firing. The subsequent run has at most one tracked position. To see this, the only remaining gate capable of increasing the count is $f$. It cannot become enabled while a tracked position is pending. If both its operands are on the same line, the firing that supplies the last operand must also emit any pending tracked position, since the preceding tracked count was at most one and was consumed. This tracked position must be the center of that line, which would give the full raw support of the line in the point singleton's closure. If the two operands are on distinct lines, emitting the last operand with a tracked position would already give the full raw support of that operand's line. If $f$ has one line operand and one point operand, the line operand cannot be emitted with a tracked position for the same reason. Its emission therefore leaves no tracked position, and the run must fire $f$ at once or stop. The argument also covers an operand supplied directly by $g$, when there is no prior tracked position to consume.

With at most one tracked position, there is at most one possible continuation. A good gate needs the leaf for the offered center. A raw-unreachable singleton cannot start a gate by itself. When no tracked position remains, only $f$ can continue. The first destination containing good leaves has no tracked position and cannot supply a line-side operand of $f$. It therefore ends the run. In the mixed case an already supplied line operand of an unfired $f$ cannot keep the run pending while other gates fire, as its emission would have cleared the sole tracked position. Thus every point singleton needed for a clean missing edge follows the same sequence from $g$ to a common leaf destination $T$. A missing guard can stop the sequence but cannot change its next gate.

The leaves of $T$ lie in one raw point support. They can include at most one missing line at each $x$, by the common-neighbor bound. Hence the clean missing edges use at least two distinct points of $S$ that all reach $T$.

At least one of the clean missing lines has a good gate whose destination accepts by itself. For a clean line $\ell$, its three retained tests start with $h_\ell$ because the points lie outside $S$ and the line supplies no operand of $f$. After $h_\ell$, their common baseline is its destination. Beyond that baseline, the tests add pairwise disjoint sets of good leaves, with $L_\ell$ consumed. They can additionally supply a raw operand of $f$ only in the mixed case. The positions of $g$ are inert because it is earlier than $h_\ell$. If $f$ fires on the baseline, at most two tracked positions are left to respond to the raw leaves of these tests, and any added operand of $f$ is inert. If $f$ does not fire on the baseline, at most one tracked position is left and $f$ can contribute at most one first difference, unless a single point-side operand of $f$ is present at two or more of the retained points. In that exception the unique mixed attachment of that operand must share the label $L_\ell$, so it can be an exception at at most one line. In the absence of a point-side operand there is no exception, and in the mixed case there are at least three clean missing lines. A rejecting baseline cannot accommodate all three retained tests outside the exception. This proves the claim.

Take such a line $\ell$ and a different clean missing point $x'$ in $S$. It is a nonedge because $\ell$ has only its missing point in $S$. The singleton at $x'$ reaches the common $T$, which contains $L_\ell$. Adding the raw line fires $h_\ell$ and accepts, a contradiction.

## Both gates have point-side sets

Now every line is clean. No singleton point reaches any center, by raw-support dominance. So only $g_1,g_2$ and gates with two raw-unreachable operands can fire on point singletons. Order $g_1,g_2$ chronologically. If $A_i$ is the set of points on whose singleton $g_i$ actually fires, then $A_1=S_1$ and $A_2\subseteq S_1\cup S_2$. Outside $S_1$, no gate can fire before $g_2$ on such a singleton. Choose lines $c_i$ with $S_i\subseteq N_H(c_i)$, allowing any choice when $S_i$ is empty.

On any true test $(x,\ell)$, the first difference from singleton $x$ must be $h_\ell$, and the singleton closure must already contain $L_\ell$. After this first extra firing the test is equivalent to the closure of the point singleton together with the destination of $h_\ell$. If that destination were contained in a raw support $D(v)$, every neighbor $x$ of $\ell$ would also give acceptance on $(x,v)$. The wire order excludes $v=\ell$ because $h_\ell$ cannot emit its own center. A point $v$ gives only nonedges with the points $x$, and another line contains at most one of the four neighbors of $\ell$. Therefore $h_\ell$ must emit the output directly or emit a singleton raw-unreachable operand $q$. In the latter case the mate of $q$ must be present in the closure of every neighbor's singleton.

Call a line direct if its good gate emits the output. A leaf destination from any gate firing on point singletons can contribute a missing edge at at most one direct line. In fact, if the destination contains leaves for two direct lines, every point singleton receiving it must be adjacent to both lines. The destination belongs to a raw point support, so that supporting point is also adjacent to both. By the common-neighbor bound it is the only point that can receive this destination, and the destination supplies none of its missing leaves. For a fixed direct line, at most one missing point lies in each $S_i$, because $S_i$ consists of assigned leaves of $c_i$.

Consider the destinations of the two nonadmissible gates on the singleton runs. A destination used there is either a subset of a raw point support or a raw-unreachable singleton, since neither a center nor the output can be reached. A gate that never fires on any singleton contributes nothing.

- If neither gate emits a raw-unreachable position on singletons, all lines are direct. The destination of $g_1$ contributes at most one missing edge, since $A_1=S_1$. The destination of $g_2$ contributes at most two, since $A_2\subseteq S_1\cup S_2$. There are no other leaf emitters on these singleton runs.
- If just one gate emits a raw-unreachable position, it is the only such position ever reached on point singletons. A line that is not direct would need this position at all four of its neighbors, which must therefore lie in $S_1\cup S_2$. Such a line is one of $c_1,c_2$, because any other meets their union in at most two points. Each of $c_1,c_2$ can then have at most one missing edge, with the missing point contributed by the other set. Thus at least three missing edges have direct lines. The sole leaf emitter can contribute at most two direct missing edges by the preceding bound.
- If both gates emit raw-unreachable singletons, a missing leaf can only be obtained when these two positions are mates and their gate emits a leaf destination. Then both positions are already present in the point singleton closure on every missing edge. There is no other raw-unreachable position in that closure. A non-direct good gate could give acceptance only by supplying a new mate for one of the two positions, which is impossible since they are each other's mates. All missing lines are direct. The one leaf destination is emitted only on singletons on which both $g_1,g_2$ fire, a subset of $S_1$. It can contribute at most one missing edge.

Each case falls short of five missing edges. This finishes the saturated twelve-center exclusion.

The [matching threshold](first-star-five.md#the-matching-threshold) gives at least twelve centers. With $w$ raw-reachable positions outside the stars, $w\ge4$ would force exactly twelve centers and equality in the budget $s\ge \sigma+c-1+w$ at $\sigma\ge43$. Hence $w\le3$, so only one nonadmissible gate can have two raw-reachable operands. The other branches of the five-edge case remain open.
