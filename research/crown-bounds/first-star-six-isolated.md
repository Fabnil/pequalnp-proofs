# Six omitted edges leave no isolated line

In [L-meta-9737](https://pequalnp.com/desk/row?gap=L-meta-9737), let $H$ be the union of the admissible raw AND stars of a minimum normalized circuit of at most 58 gates. If exactly six edges are outside $H$, every line has a retained edge. This rules out the isolated-line case of the [six-deletion Hall classification](first-star-five.md#the-matching-threshold), including the case of an isolated line and an isolated point on it.

Use the [six-edge budget](first-star-six-budget.md) and its notation. Let $D(y)$ be the support reached from raw vertex $y$ along OR-only paths. It is nonempty because $y$ has a neighbor in $G$, so selecting $y$ must be able to change the output. Supposing there is an isolated line $v$, there are $k_0=1$ or $2$ isolated vertices. In the latter case the other vertex $u$ is a degree-three point on $v$, and all six omissions are incident to $u$ or $v$. In the transposed union network, let $P$ count pure erased unions in the raw ancestries and $q$ the mixed attachments, so $A=P+q$. The retained union count gives

$$
A+R+E\le B,\qquad B=17-c-k_0.
$$

For an isolated vertex, its live neighbors have disjoint projected raw supports even if some are centers. A mixed attachment propagates a common retained label, so it can reach at most one of those neighbors. It reaches raw vertices on just one physical side of the graph. Throughout, fold an OR-only fanout starting with a nonempty projected contribution whenever possible. A pure union then occurs at a wire with empty full projection. If a wire reaches just the two operands of one AND and has just one fork along its OR-only continuation, factoring saves a gate by the [earlier reduction](first-star-cover.md#spanning-in-the-three-edge-case). In particular, the raw support of an isolated vertex containing both operands of an AND needs at least two pure unions.

## The erased components at equality

Each weak component of the pure erased network has at least one outlet, counting isolated raw outputs and inputs to mixed attachments with multiplicity. Let $r_i$ be the number of outlets of component $i$. The sum of its undirected cycle ranks over all components is $\kappa=\#\text{components}-(w-P)\ge0$. Consequently

$$
\sum_i(r_i-1)=q+k_0-(w-P)-\kappa=\epsilon-\kappa .
$$

For a noncenter $v_0$, let $X$ consist of its missing noncenter neighbors. The [first-difference count](first-star-six-budget.md#a-bound-that-allows-slack) gives $|X|\le M+\Delta-\kappa$. Indeed, an erased operand used as a first-difference witness covers only neighbors reached by its component's outlets. If a component supplies $t\ge1$ witnesses, they cover at most $r_i$ members of $X$, compared with a budget of $t+r_i-1$. With no witnesses there is no contribution. The other witnesses are waiting good centers. The singleton fires $J$ fully raw-reachable nonadmissible gates and ends with at most $J+R+E$ tracked positions. Adding the $M-J$ unfired gates and the outlet surplus gives the bound.

At $|X|=M+\Delta$, all steps in this count are tight. In particular $\kappa=0$, $R+E+\epsilon=\Delta$, and every component with more than one outlet supplies one witness and reaches different members of $X$ at all of its outlets. No two witnesses can use the same component. These statements apply for either choice of an absent operand of an unfired fully raw-reachable gate. If $v_0$ is isolated, its raw outlet cannot reach any member of $X$, so its component has just that one outlet. Every erased position it receives is then private to $v_0$. At $\epsilon=0$ the same privacy holds at every isolated vertex directly, since all components have one outlet.

## Covers when a line is isolated

The [budget and Hall analysis](first-star-six-budget.md#cover-sizes-at-six-deletions) leaves $11\le c\le13$, and at most four erased raw-reachable operand positions in each of these cases. At $c=11$ all eleven live lines are centers. At $c=12$ the [incidence-pair count](first-star-six-budget.md#matching-deficiency-with-twelve-centers) allows omitting $k=0,1,11$ live lines from the centers. That geometric count uses only the two deletions away from $v$. With $k=0$ or $1$, at most one point center lies on $v$. With $k=11$, the sole unchosen point is isolated and all its line neighbors are noncenters.

At $c=13$ the $k$ omitted live lines must retain at least $4k-2$ incidences with the $k+2$ chosen points. The number of incidences is at most $k+2+\binom{k}{2}$, since $d\le1+\binom d2$ for each chosen point of incidence degree $d$. This excludes $k=2,3,4$. The bound $\binom d2\ge3d-6$ gives $\binom k2\ge6k-18$, excluding $k=5,6,7,8$. At $k=9$ there is equality, so every pair of omitted lines meets at a chosen point in $H$. The two unchosen points each have at most one original neighbor among the omitted lines and at least two among the three other lines. A degree-four unchosen point would use all three and share two with the other point, contrary to the common-neighbor bound. Two degree-three points share no line and would need four different lines. Thus $k=0,1,10,11$. At $k=0$ or $1$ at most two point centers lie on $v$. When $k=1$, the omitted live line uses at least two of the three chosen points and has at most one point in common with $v$. At $k=10$ or $11$ there is at most one line center.

## At most three erased positions

There is a unique nonadmissible gate $g$ with both operands $a,b$ raw-reachable. It must fire initially on every missed edge. Suppose first that only $v$ is isolated. If $D(v)$ lacks $b$, its four neighbors need separate mixed attachments carrying $b$. When $c\ge12$ this either exceeds $B$ or uses all four unions with no pure union, leaving no $a$ on the edges off $v$. When $c=11$, there is room for at most one pure union after the four attachments. If any of the four carries both $a,b$, that sole pure union gives a full wire support $\{a,b\}$ and one fork, which can be factored. Otherwise a fifth attachment is needed to supply $a$ off $v$. There are then exactly five mixed attachments, $w=2$, and $R=E=0$. The four neighbors cannot all supply $a$ through one attachment, so $D(v)=\{a\}$. On the four tests at $v$, after $g$ fires the state is its destination plus disjoint sets of good leaves. If the destination accepts by itself, all raw firings of $g$ lead to acceptance, making $g$ admissible. Otherwise the destination has at most one track and no later firing can increase that count. At most one of the four neighbors can supply the missing leaf for a waiting center, a contradiction.

Thus $a,b\in D(v)$. There are $P\ge2$ pure unions and $q\ge1$ mixed attachments, since some missed edge is off $v$. The singleton at $v$ fires $g$ and leaves at most $1+R+E$ pending tracked positions. Its missing noncenter neighbors can add disjoint good leaf sets and possibly the third erased position. The latter can reach at most $q$ of them and is read by a single waiting raw-unreachable operand. So the number of noncenter neighbors served is at most

$$
1+R+E+(q-1)\le B-2 .
$$

This excludes $c=11,12$, with four and at least three noncenter neighbors respectively. It also excludes the $c=13$ covers with $k=0,1$, which have at least two such neighbors.

For $c=13$ with $k=10,11$, we have $B=3$, $P=2$, $q=1$ and $R=E=0$. To include the center neighbors as well, count the pending tracks together with the target leaves $L_x$ of center neighbors $x$ of $v$ that are ever emitted on singleton $v$. Any destination contains at most one such leaf. A nonsingleton destination lies inside one raw support, and a line different from $v$ shares at most one of its points. A destination emitting a new target leaf and a new tracked position must contain a line center. There is at most one such center, and its position can be new only once. Apart from that possible extra position, a firing adds at most one to the count before consumption. Every firing except $g$ consumes a track. The resulting combined count is at most two. The first difference on $(v,x)$ must pair an added leaf with a waiting center, an added third erased position with its waiting mate, or an added center $C_x$ with an emitted target leaf $L_x$. The raw supports are disjoint on retained labels, and the one attachment lets the third erased position reach at most one neighbor. Thus each counted item serves at most one neighbor, fewer than four in total.

If both $v$ and the point $u$ are isolated, $B\le4$. If they each lack an operand of $g$, the three live point neighbors of $v$ and two live line neighbors of $u$ need five different mixed attachments. If only $v$ lacks an operand, three mixed attachments and two pure unions for $u$ again need five. Hence $a,b\in D(v)$. If they also lie in $D(u)$, the two supports are nested because there are at most three erased positions. This contradicts [raw-support dominance](first-star-five-saturation.md#setup-and-raw-support-dominance). Otherwise the two pure unions for $v$ and two mixed attachments for the line neighbors of $u$ give $A\ge4$. Equality requires $c=11$, with no erased positions at the three live point neighbors of $v$ and $R=E=0$. Their disjoint leaf sets need three waiting centers on singleton $v$, which has at most one.

## Four erased positions at eleven or twelve centers

Choose an isolated vertex $v_0$ as follows. For $c=11$ take $v_0=v$, with $m=|X|=4$ missing noncenter neighbors. For $c=12$ with $k=0,1$ again take $v_0=v$, with $m\ge3$. For $c=12$ with $k=11$ take $v_0=u$, with $m=3$. Since $M\le2$ and $\Delta\le13-c$, the only possibility is $M=2$ and $m=2+\Delta$. In particular $m=3$ at $c=12$, so with $k=0,1$ exactly one point center lies on $v$. The two fully raw-reachable nonadmissible gates exhaust the four erased positions.

The equality count makes $D(v_0)$ private to $v_0$. Some missed edge lies off $v_0$ and needs a gate with both operands outside $D(v_0)$. Denote this gate by $g_2$ with operands $d,e$, and the other gate by $g_1$ with operands $a,b$. The nonempty $D(v_0)$ lies in $\{a,b\}$. If it contains both, it needs at least two pure unions, but a connected component with two sources and at least two binary unions has positive cycle rank. Thus $D(v_0)=\{a\}$ after relabeling. No gate fires on this singleton and it has no pending track. Equality now gives $R=E=0$ and $\epsilon=m-2$.

Each live member of $X$ must supply a missing operand via a mixed attachment. Thus $q\ge m-(k_0-1)$. Since $A=4-k_0+\epsilon=m+2-k_0$, at most one pure union is available. The two ways to start a test are to get $b$ from $x$ and fire $g_1$, or to get both $d,e$ from $x$ and fire $g_2$. At equality both ways are needed and their sets of neighbors are disjoint. This follows by using $b$ and either $d$ or $e$ as the two erased witnesses. In particular the component of $b$ differs from each of the components of $d,e$.

Suppose $g_2$ can serve $j\ge2$ neighbors. If $d,e$ are in one component, the sole pure union combines them, making a wire with full support $\{d,e\}$ and one fork. Factoring saves a gate. If they are in distinct components, each needs at least $j$ outlets, and the component of $b$ needs at least $m-j$. The total surplus is at least $2(j-1)+(m-j-1)>m-2$. Hence $g_2$ serves exactly one neighbor $y$. The $b$ component uses all $\epsilon=m-2$ extra outlets on the other $m-1$ neighbors. Each of $d,e$ has a single outlet reaching $y$, and neither can reach the other $m-1$ neighbors.

Let $T$ be the destination of $g_1$. Its run alone rejects, since acceptance would make $g_1$ admissible by the same raw-pair argument as above. On the $m-1$ tests at $v_0$ that fire $g_1$, the only additions to $T$ are disjoint sets of good leaves. Each requires a different waiting center in the terminal run from $T$. Initially there is at most one track, and only $g_2$ can increase the count. This excludes $m-1=3$, the case $c=11$.

When $m-1=2$, both terminal tracks must be good centers. The initial destination contains one track and every firing in the run emits a track, with $g_2$ contributing the one increase. A raw-unreachable operand would either remain pending instead of a good center or be consumed together with another raw-unreachable operand, losing one track. The destination $T$ must already supply both $d,e$. It lies in a raw support if nonsingleton, so no good gate has both inputs there. A gate with two raw-unreachable operands cannot start there either. The only way to continue is to fire $g_2$ at once.

If $v_0=u$ and all centers are points, $y$ is a line. Since $d,e$ each reach raw $y$ through one outlet, they reach no raw point. Then $T$ cannot contain a center along with $d,e$. If $v_0=v$, the one chosen point $z$ on $v$ cannot receive $d,e$, because $y$ is another point on $v$ and their projected supports are disjoint. An outlet reaching $y$ can only reach points. Thus $T$ can have a center along with $d,e$ only when there is a second chosen point $z'$ off $v$. This is the $k=1$ cover, and $z,z'$ share the omitted live line. We have $T\subseteq D(z')$. Both pending centers at the end must be line centers to read leaves from the point neighbors. The point center $C_{z'}$ in $T$ must therefore be consumed by its own gate, requiring emission of $L_{z'}$. That emission also has a line center $C_\ell$. All outlets of $b$ serve points of $X$, and $d,e$ reach the point $y$. Thus $a$ is the only erased position that reaches any raw line, and it is private to $v$. No chosen line contains both $z,z'$. It follows that $D(\ell)=\{C_\ell,L_{z'}\}$. The ordinary closure of singleton $z'$ contains $T$ and therefore reaches both positions, contradicting raw-support dominance.

## Four erased positions at thirteen centers

Here $\Delta=0$ and the erased components each have one outlet. The positions of $D(v)$ are private, and some missed edge away from $v$ needs a fully raw-reachable gate avoiding them. This leaves at most two positions in $D(v)$. A two-position isolated support consisting of both operands of another gate would need two pure unions and hence a cycle. Therefore every fully raw-reachable gate is missing an operand at $v$. Each such operand can reach at most one of the four neighbors of $v$ through its one outlet, even counting center neighbors. Only those gates can fire first on the four missing edges at $v$, and there are at most two of them.

Thus $H$ meets every line. The [two-point follow-up](first-star-six-two-points.md) also excludes two isolated points.
