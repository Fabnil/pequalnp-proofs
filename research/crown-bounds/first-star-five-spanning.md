# Five uncovered edges force the star subgraph to span

In [L-meta-9737](https://pequalnp.com/desk/row?gap=L-meta-9737), suppose a minimum normalized circuit of size at most 58 has exactly five edges outside the union $H$ of its admissible raw AND stars. Then $H$ spans all 25 vertices. In particular, the [single-gate restriction](first-star-five-saturation.md) holds without assuming anything about isolated lines. Exactly one nonadmissible AND has both operands raw-reachable and it fires first on all five missing edges.

## Counting the supports at an isolated vertex

Suppose $v$ is isolated in $H$ and let $X=N_G(v)$. There can be only one isolated vertex with five deletions. Distinct points would require at least six deletions, as would a line and a point even if they share an edge, and two lines would require eight. The 24 live raw supports therefore require at least $\sigma+c-24$ retained unions, where $\sigma$ counts oriented star incidences and $c$ distinct centers. There are at most 34 unions in all.

The projected raw supports of the neighbors in $X$ are pairwise disjoint. Center labels belong to a unique raw vertex and a common leaf label would give two neighbors a second common neighbor besides $v$. A mixed attachment that carries an erased position into the live raw supports consequently reaches at most one member of $X$.

Suppose there is a unique nonadmissible AND $g$ with both operands $a,b$ raw-reachable. It fires first on all edges at $v$. If one of $a,b$ is missing from $D(v)$, then that operand must occur at every member of $X$, through a different mixed attachment each time. If $D(v)$ contains both $a,b$, it requires at least two pure erased unions. This is immediate if it also contains a third position. For $D(v)=\{a,b\}$ with only one pure union feeding it, the [one-fork reduction](first-star-cover.md#spanning-in-the-three-edge-case) saves a gate. There is also at least one mixed attachment, since five missing edges cannot all be incident to $v$ and $g$ must fire first on the remaining edges.

We use the [first-difference comparison](first-star-spanning.md#support-and-comparison-bounds-at-an-isolated-vertex) with the rejecting singleton at $v$. Track good center positions and raw-unreachable operand positions. The singleton starts with no tracked positions. If $J$ gates with two raw-reachable erased operands fire on it, and every destination adds at most one tracked position, at most $J$ tracked positions remain. The other gates each consume a tracked position.

As in the [four-edge proof](first-star-four-exclusion.md#accounting-for-the-last-union), writing $R$ for repeated center positions and $E$ for unions unused by raw supports increases the union count by $R+E$. If $R+E\le1$, every destination adds at most one tracked position except for a possible fixed pair. Repeated emission of that pair adds no new positions. Thus at most $J+1$ tracked positions remain in this case.

## An isolated point

Suppose $v$ is a point. The [matching bound](first-star-five.md#the-matching-threshold) gives $c\ge12$, so at most three erased unions are available. Since no line is isolated, the [five-edge bound](first-star-five-saturation.md) gives $w\le3$ and a unique $g$. If $D(v)$ lacks one of $a,b$, it needs at least three mixed attachments for the missing position. At degree four this already exceeds the allowance. At degree three it uses all three erased unions, each with that one position as its erased operand, with no pure union to combine it with the other position. The other operand would then be unavailable outside $v$, leaving some missing edge without an initial gate.

Thus $D(v)$ contains $a,b$. Two pure unions and one mixed attachment exhaust the allowance. In particular $c=12$, $\sigma=43$, every union is used for raw supports, and there is one gate per center. The sole mixed attachment must carry both $a,b$ to live raw supports for the missing edges away from $v$. Its live raw set $S$ has a common retained label. It also supplies every erased position that occurs outside $v$. If $w=3$, call the remaining erased position $d$. It can occur at at most one neighbor in $X$, and only matters for the first difference if its raw-unreachable mate is waiting in the singleton's terminal support. With $J=1$, there is at most one waiting tracked position.

The [cover count for twelve centers](first-star-five.md#every-center-is-a-line), before using the noncenter constraint there, leaves $k=0,1,2,11,12$ omitted lines.

- For $k=0$, all neighbors of $v$ are line centers. In the singleton at $v$, count together the pending tracked positions and distinct leaves $L_\ell$ ever emitted for $\ell\in X$. Any destination containing such a target leaf has no tracked position and can contain at most one target leaf. Its supporting point cannot be $v$ and has at most one common line with $v$. Thus the combined count stays at most one, since only $g$ can increase it. The first difference on $(v,\ell)$ must use either the target leaf $L_\ell$ or the waiting raw-unreachable mate of $d$. This cannot accommodate the at least three neighbors.
- For $k=2$, all five deletions must be on the two omitted lines. Their incidences to the two chosen points total at most three, and all three must be retained. No point can then lose more than two edges.
- For $k=11$, the two unchosen points include $v$ and another point $u$. The omitted edges at $v,u$ are disjoint, and $u$ can have at most one retained edge, to the chosen line. To stay within five deletions both points must have degree three and $u$ must retain its edge to the chosen line. The degree-three points have no common line, so none of $v$'s neighbors is a center. For $k=12$ there are no line centers either. In both cases the neighbors add only pairwise disjoint good leaf sets and possibly $d$, which is supplied at most once. Each needs a distinct waiting tracked position in the singleton at $v$, which has at most one.

For $k=1$, write $\ell_0$ for the omitted line and $z$ for the sole point center. The line $\ell_0$ retains just its edge to $z$, so it misses three edges. To fit these and the edges of $v$ into five deletions, $v$ has degree three and is one of the missing neighbors of $\ell_0$. The two missed edges away from $v$ both use $\ell_0$. Their point endpoints have disjoint good leaf sets since they share the noncenter $\ell_0$. They cannot both belong to $S$, so $S$ is on the line side and contains $\ell_0$. The only retained label at $\ell_0$ is $L_z$, and all lines in $S$ are assigned leaves of $z$.

The two other neighbors $\ell_1,\ell_2$ of $v$ are centers and do not contain $z$, because $v,z$ already share $\ell_0$. They are outside $S$ and have only their respective center positions as raw supports. The singleton at $v$ must reach both $L_{\ell_1},L_{\ell_2}$ to enable the first difference on those two positive tests. A support that emits either leaf lies in one raw point support. It cannot be the support at $z$, and hence has no tracked position. It cannot include both leaves, since the supporting point and $v$ cannot share two lines. After such a leaf emission the continuation has no tracked position and stops, as $g$ has already fired. It cannot supply the other leaf. This excludes the last isolated-point case.

## An isolated line

Suppose instead $v$ is a line. There are four missing edges at $v$ and exactly one elsewhere. Restrict an original shift matching that avoids the one other deletion to the eleven live lines. This gives $c\ge11$.

If $c=11$, all eleven live lines must be centers. Omitting $k>0$ would require retaining at least $4k-1$ incidences on $k$ point centers, so the incidence-pair count gives $\binom{k}{2}\ge6k-3$, impossible for $1\le k\le11$. If $c=12$, the analogous count on $k+1$ points gives $\binom{k}{2}\ge6k-9$, impossible for $2\le k\le11$. Omitting one live line would require three retained incidences on only two chosen points. Thus $c=12$ consists of the eleven live lines and one point center.

### At least twelve centers

With $c\ge13$, at most two erased unions are available and $w\le3$, so $g$ is unique. A missing operand in $D(v)$ would require four mixed attachments. Both operands in $D(v)$ require two pure unions and a further mixed one for the missing edge away from $v$. Either way exceeds the allowance.

For $c=12$ there are at most three erased unions and $w\le4$. If $w=4$ the budget is tight. The [noncenter neighbor bound](first-star-five.md#a-constraint-on-missing-neighbors-of-noncenters) uses only tight erasure and the common-neighbor property, so it applies to an isolated line as well. At least three neighbors are noncenters and there are at most two nonadmissible gates whose two operands are raw-reachable. This violates that bound.

If $w\le3$, the gate $g$ is unique and $D(v)$ must contain both operands. Two pure unions and a mixed one exhaust the allowance, with no repeated centers and no off-raw union. At most one tracked position remains on singleton $v$. The at least three noncenter neighbors add pairwise disjoint good leaves, and only a possible third erased position can matter after $g$. That position has just one mixed attachment and thus is supplied by at most one neighbor. Each first difference requires its own waiting tracked position, which is impossible.

### Eleven centers

Now all four neighbors of $v$ are noncenters, $\sigma=43$, and there are at most four erased unions. The gate bound gives $w\le5$. With $w=5$ the count is tight and the noncenter neighbor bound again rules out four missing noncenter neighbors.

For $w\le3$ the gate $g$ is unique. If $D(v)$ lacks an operand of $g$, all four erased unions must be mixed attachments carrying that same operand. The other operand cannot reach any live raw vertex and the missing edge off $v$ cannot trigger $g$. Thus $D(v)$ contains both operands. The two pure unions and one necessary mixed attachment spend at least three of the at most four erased unions. Together with the retained count this gives $R+E\le1$, so at most two tracked positions remain on the singleton. Each waiting center can serve at most one of the four raw leaf sets. A third erased position $d$, if present, may serve up to two neighbors, since at most two mixed attachments remain after the pure unions. This uses one waiting position, namely the raw-unreachable mate of $d$. In total the two waiting positions cannot serve four neighbors.

It remains to consider $w=4$. The erasure lower count requires at least $w-1=3$ erased unions. Write $P$ for the number of pure erased unions in the raw ancestries and $q$ for the number of mixed attachments, so $A=P+q$ is three or four. In the pure erased network there are $w+P$ vertices and $2P$ edges, and hence at least $w-P$ weak components. Each component feeds at least one of the $q+1$ designated outputs, consisting of the empty raw support of $v$ and the empty operands of the mixed attachments. Count designated outputs with multiplicity if an operand is shared. There are at most $A+1-w$ more designations than components.

If $A=3$, every component has just one designation. Hence any erased position reaches at most one neighbor of $v$. We have $R+E\le1$, so if $J$ of the $M\le2$ gates with two raw-reachable operands fired on the singleton, at most $J+1$ tracked positions remain. For the first difference on a neighbor test, either one of these tracked positions gets its mate from the neighbor, or one of the $M-J$ unfired fully raw-reachable gates gets a fixed missing operand from the neighbor. Each possibility serves at most one neighbor, for a total of at most $M+1\le3$.

If $A=4$, then $R=E=0$ and at most $J$ tracked positions remain. At most one pure component can have two designations and none can have three. If its two designations are both mixed attachments, they reach at most two fixed neighbors. Thus any shared erased position among the four neighbors is shared only by that same pair. Each first-difference possibility again serves at most one neighbor, except that several possibilities can serve the same fixed pair. That can add at most one neighbor to the total of $J+(M-J)=M$. The total is still at most three.

This excludes an isolated line, and thus proves that $H$ spans. The [cover analysis](first-star-five-cover.md) restricts the branch with twelve centers to all line centers and a singleton point trigger. The existence of a circuit at size 58 remains open.
